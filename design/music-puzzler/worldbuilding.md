# The Lumen Reach

The Quiet Bell keeps its Pocket Pixel presentation and gains a retrofuture high-fantasy setting. People inhabit floating stone islands, root bridges and light canals. They use crystal cells, curved phosphor displays and beautiful old machines whose original makers are gone. Much of the world’s technology is understood through tending, listening and repair.

The public bell coordinates mail birds, water pumps and ferry safety lamps. It sends a call and waits for a reply meaning “I got your message” or “the landing is safe.” A loose return connection stops those replies reaching the bell. Local crystal cells still power lamps, birds and test signals. Mail and supplies wait, gardens need water, and ferries cannot confirm a safe landing. These are ongoing practical problems; there is no countdown or hidden villain.

A clockwork bird brings an undelivered letter back through forgotten service perches. The charging perch and the bird’s warm metal carrying clip heat the wax seal. The returned letter on the desk is separate from Nell’s bag of note tiles, which Mira uses at rhythm locks. Early rooms present evidence; later rooms explain the failed return connection and the bird’s route. Every successful ending recaps the explanation and its repair, so a secret route is never required to understand the main story.

The garden ending restores nearby water controls and home deliveries. The school ending restores the shared music room and its training connection. The public-bell ending repairs the main return connection for mail, water and ferry checks across the town. One secret restores the birds’ own reply route; another restores Iris’s listening window so her friend, alive on another island, can answer. The empty chair is for a future visit. Local endings say that the wider system still needs work.

The complete route opens only after the journal records all eleven earlier outcomes: the five successful story endings, including both secrets, and all six failed runs. Restarting resets note supplies and physical repairs; it preserves the map and what the friends learned. The Meeting of Routes contains old service controls that reach the smaller connections, and The Listening Heart joins them to the public bell’s return wire. In this final trip, the party connects every service, tests all five replies, and reaches A Town Answering Together. Homes receive water and deliveries, ferries confirm safe landings, the music room teaches music and repair, birds use their own reply horn, and Iris’s friend arrives safely in town for tea. All four friends finish together. The existing twelve depot beats pay three doors exactly, with no new supply and no leftover tiles. The complete ending adds a twelfth recorded outcome and never counts toward its own unlock.

Write for fifth graders: concrete things first, short sentences, clear causes and consequences. Explain an unfamiliar machine when students encounter it. Keep the fantasy in floating islands, lively characters, memorable objects and daily life rather than vague metaphors. Each room’s brief narrator passage establishes the setting, problem and immediate goal before the party enters. Conversations and optional objects carry the discoveries; inspections do not automatically search for hidden notes.

## The travelling party

Nell leads. She is wise, thoughtful and well versed in the Reach's machinery and history, rarely surprised and a little impatient. She gives the group a purpose, checks their conclusions and brings a wandering discussion back to the task. Mira often gets distracted, so Nell gives her a brief, gently firm correction and points her back to the clue.

Mira is the observant, plucky, slightly brash heroine, with the biggest personality in the group. Her imagination is whimsical, her replies are bold, and food is frequently on her mind. Her jokes start with something she has actually noticed; hunger should vary her voice rather than replace it. Food, shiny machinery and curious details often distract her, but she can find useful clues while wandering. Her recurring catchphrase is “Shimmy shimmy!” Use it at lively discoveries and celebrations, rather than in every reply.

Iris is pragmatic, investigative and fearless. Her academic delivery is dry, droll and emotionally level, including when everyone else is startled. She produces a magnifying glass to examine evidence, distinguishes observations from guesses and lets an understated remark carry the joke. Her practical, rational view regularly clashes with Pip’s emotional reactions; she answers a fearful guess with evidence instead of mocking him.

Pip is curious and excitable, but afraid of almost everything. A promising discovery can send him running before he thinks about what might be waiting there. His startled stumble, harmless fall and quick recovery are playful character acting; he gets back up and continues contributing. He leads with his feelings, so he and matter-of-fact Iris often disagree. His concern for people and birds can reveal a need that her analysis misses.

Dialogue sounds conversational and contemporary, using contractions and natural connections such as and, but and so. Keep each character’s voice clear without stiff phrases like ‘I shall’ or ‘let us.’

Room openings let the party move and react together. They investigate different physical details while their conversation connects those discoveries. Nell's leadership, Iris's unruffled scrutiny, Pip's nervous enthusiasm and Mira's lively observations should sound like four people sharing a room.

## A lived world in every room

Every room needs four clear answers: who uses it, what they do here, what has changed, and what the place suggests beyond its visible walls. Give it one readable landmark, a daily habit, a trace of an older use and a route clue or promise. A folded wing wrap, repaired cup or thumb-worn button often says more than another glowing machine.

Show the connection between magic, machinery and work: a root feeds a copper pipe, the pipe wakes a crystal manifold, and the manifold waters somebody’s seedlings. Preserve warm people and practical repairs alongside grand architecture. Keep one or two mysteries unanswered rather than explaining the entire civilization in each inspection.

Garden rooms use jade, amber sap, cream ceramic and living roots. Workshop rooms use teal enamel, copper, blue star-glass and adapted instruments. Canal rooms use indigo, pearl stone, silvered water and weathered brass. Moonstone arches, brass route rings and phosphor glass connect the branches. Apply the 2/3 orthographic view to every room, furnishing and character. Show tops and front faces with parallel edges, rectangular tabletops and constant floor spacing; objects do not taper or recede toward a vanishing point. Leave generous clear floor between the smaller furnishings and travelling companions; use strong pixel clusters, simple faces and warm dark outlines. Avoid religious or occult signs, grim violence and writing on the floor.

## Scope and implementation

[worldbuilding.json](worldbuilding.json) gives every released place an occupation, material palette, landmark, daily habit, trace of the past, unresolved question, route clue and inspection props. All nineteen authored dungeon rooms and seven ending locations are playable: three regular endings, two secret endings, the complete ending and the empty-ferry dead end. The overgrown arch, old aviary and copied waterway are paid detours within the dungeon, with the same existing artwork and clues. The depot, garden porch, bird workshop and canal storehouse keep their established conversations and observations. `games/rhythm-blocks/campaign-content.mjs` adds the later rooms and outcomes, with Nell, Iris and Pip responding to each location and Oren remaining at the canal storehouse. There are eighty-one environmental observations across the campaign, including Fred the wolf spider hidden in the birds’ attic.

The three porch gates now enter the actual garden paths, workshop crossroads and water clock. There are no continuation-preview barriers in the released campaign. The two detours contain explicit once-only searches beneath the flower stone and inside the hollow perch. Discovering those supplies reveals their physical gates; the gate phrases spend the newly found notes while the original final answer remains in the shared bag. Every successful outcome spends all carried notes. Paid keys are irreversible: extra gates can leave an empty bag before the final lock or leave the wrong note kinds for the next gate. Wrong guesses cost nothing, and draft Undo stays free. A failed run can still be explored for clues before restarting; the discovered journal and map persist, while notes and pickups reset.

The economy in `dungeon.json` stays authoritative. Lore inspections grant no notes, paid doors, party promises or outcomes. They can preserve observations in the journal and allude to other possibilities, while the chart continues to show only entered rooms and travelled connections. Hidden sources and gates must retain their existing once-only claims, reveal conditions and typed budgets.

Each room remains a starting point for a later story and detail pass through the developer editor. The verified campaign keeps nineteen dungeon rooms, twenty-eight paid doors, seven once-only supplies and seven terminal outcomes. Five additional resource failures give twelve recorded outcomes in all, including the complete ending. Three intentional paid detours teach note planning by producing exhausted or wrong-type failures before a successful ending. All four friends remain together for the complete route. Companion settling, departures and a separate drop-off achievement remain future design work; the present campaign does not award those unfinished achievements.
