# The Quiet Bell

The Quiet Bell is the dungeon-puzzle evolution of Rhythm Blocks. The complete checked flow chart is playable at `games/rhythm-blocks/`: **14 dungeon rooms, 9 walkable outcome locations, 22 rhythm doors and 7 once-only note sources**. Choose **Games → The Quiet Bell** in the arcade. The URL, arcade game ID `rhythm-blocks`, host key `rhythmBlocks` and same-origin return protocol stay stable. It uses static files without a runtime service or package installation.

Every location has distinct Pocket Pixel artwork, room-specific companion conversations and things to inspect. The setting is Lumen Reach: floating islands connected by an inherited star-post network, living roots, light canals and crystal machinery. The garden route passes through shared root courts and household relays; the workshop explores bird couriers, listening classrooms and a sheltered aviary; the canal follows water clocks to the evening bell tower. Regular endings restore a household, a school or the public bell. Two hidden routes lead to quiet and bird-post endings. Four dead ends have their own explorable settings and repair clues. This is a complete first narrative pass for subsequent room-by-room editing.

All rooms and sprites use orthographic 2/3 projection: tops and south faces are visible, parallel edges stay parallel and floor spacing does not shrink with distance. New artwork is bundled under `assets/rooms/`; original opening scenes remain in `assets/parcel-depot.webp`, `garden-porch.webp`, `workshop-porch.webp` and `canal-porch.webp`. Mira, Nell, Iris, Pip and Oren have four-direction walking artwork with idle and alternating step poses. They turn at path corners, stop in the facing direction, and retain that direction after the scene. Measured boot anchors, furniture-base collision and foreground silhouettes keep movement grounded. The camera shows the room height, following Mira sideways on narrow screens. The room fills the viewport without document scrolling; inventory, settings and contextual controls leave exploration clear.

Opening the game plays a full-screen, flat scrolling story introduction with the three authored Lumen Reach paragraphs. Escape or the visible Skip intro button goes straight to the room entrance; the same key does not skip that entrance too. The crawl pauses in the background and fits small screens; reduced motion offers a stationary scrollable reading view with Continue. Start over plays it again, and Settings → Replay story intro replays it without changing the run. Applying a developer project and moving between rooms keep their usual direct flow. The intro never changes notes, journal discoveries, saved maps or project edits.

Walk with arrows/WASD, click or tap the floor, or use the on-screen arrows. Movement follows four directions: up, down, left and right. When two direction keys are held, the most recently pressed takes priority; releasing it resumes the previous direction. Floor navigation and cutscene paths turn at right angles too. Selecting a person, prop, chest or door makes Mira approach and interact; E or Space interacts nearby. Nell, Iris and Pip are talkable throughout all 23 locations. Oren remains in the canal storehouse. Each place has local stories, route clues and inventory advice. The worldbuilding guides in `design/music-puzzler/worldbuilding.json` and `worldbuilding.md` describe all locations and their inspection props. Seventy-one observations and the companions’ route replies become discovered journal entries without revealing untravelled chart connections.

Mira starts with an empty satchel. Accept Nell’s eight-note parcel and open the depot chest for the missing half note. Gifts are explicit and once-only; conversation grants nothing. The garden and workshop porches have Iris’s and Pip’s gifts, and the canal storehouse has a stamped delivery chest. These restore the entry phrase for the next lock. Two alcoves contain hidden caches: inspect the relevant object, then choose its Search action. Searching reveals the corresponding hidden gate; merely inspecting it does not collect notes. Unfound caches do not appear in the inventory ledger.

Every published door requires its exact ordered four-beat rhythm, beginning with a sounded note. Hear the lock or a resident’s example, arrange your notes, then open it. Playback gives a four-beat count-off, then a quieter metronome beneath the rhythm with the first beat accented; rests remain silent in the note voice. A different four-beat phrase spends nothing. Only the accepted key is consumed; unused notes travel onward. Quarter notes, paired eighths, four sixteenths and quarter rests fill one beat; half notes/rests fill two; dotted halves fill three; single eighths and paired sixteenths fill half a beat. Rests sound as silence. Keys 1–9 add tiles while solving, Backspace undoes, and selecting a staged note returns it to the satchel. There is no timer, combat or listening penalty.

The source economy is `design/music-puzzler/dungeon.json`, mirrored in `campaign-data.mjs`. Its independent verifier checks every reachable inventory and pickup order. All 24 authored histories reach the nine outcomes; each of the three regular and two secret endings leaves zero notes of every kind. Dead ends can retain notes. Hidden gates require their cache to have been collected even if an edited inventory can afford their key.

Every outcome is an actual walkable scene. Its Read ending control opens the narrative summary, which can be closed to keep exploring and talking. The south threshold and Revisit previous choice rewind the last entry payment and remove pickups collected beyond it, preventing source farming across branches. A staged answer can be undone freely. Start over resets run notes and pickups while preserving permanent discoveries. Revisit, reload and new runs all preserve the discovered journey.

Escape or Settings pauses the game and draws only places reached and connections travelled, accumulated across runs. There are no unknown branch placeholders or undiscovered totals. The outcomes journal records only endings and dead ends actually visited. **The Complete Score** is awarded after all 23 locations, 22 connections and 9 outcomes have been discovered across runs. The deeper ten-step companion departure routes and whole-party finales in `design/music-puzzler/replay-and-party.md` remain a separate future expansion; they are not required by this released chart.

The game uses the established device-only keys: `codaQuietBellPageOneV1` for version-3 run actions/conversation/position, `codaQuietBellJourneyV1` for observed IDs and `codaRhythmBlocksSoundV1` for sound. Reload reconstructs inventory from valid claims and paid doors rather than trusting stored balances. Version-2 opening saves and version-3 continuation-preview saves migrate into the actual rooms. Malformed histories cannot forge payments or duplicate pickups. Earlier version-1 opening saves start fresh. Falling-game scores, shared profiles, arcade progress and Notes wallet balances remain untouched. Cloud sync and currency rewards are not implemented.

The stable address checks a fresh release manifest on open or reload, then loads matching scripts, styles and artwork from an immutable content-versioned directory. Cached arcade URLs and old query parameters preserve the same origin and saves. There is no mid-game reload or browser-storage clearing. Pages publishing and CDN propagation can still take a few minutes.

`chapter.mjs` adapts the verified `campaign-data.mjs` graph for `dungeon-game.mjs`, which replays transactions and enforces hidden discovery. `campaign-content.mjs` supplies all dialogue, observations and outcome text; `campaign-scenes.mjs` supplies geometry and art-aligned targets. The original `chapter-content.mjs`, `party-content.mjs` and `scenes.mjs` remain the reused opening baseline. `journey.mjs` filters saved discoveries against the full trusted catalog, and `journey-view.mjs` renders only observed snapshots. `room.mjs`/`room-view.mjs` handle feet-based collision, pathfinding and movement-only animation. `mira.mjs` and `cast.mjs` define measured sprite anchors; `npc-sprites.mjs` selects each companion’s direction and step frame without changing their logical feet. `npc-gestures.mjs` stages the dedicated `assets/pip-forward-fall.webp` poses, preserving custom character artwork. The four companion walk sheets are `assets/nell-walk.webp`, `iris-walk.webp`, `pip-walk.webp` and `oren-walk.webp`. `audio.mjs` follows editable durations, subdivisions and tempo; `notation.mjs` supplies engraved symbols with bundled source/license information. The earlier `engine.mjs`, `story.mjs`, `opening.mjs` and `lore.mjs` remain for compatibility and regression checks.

Each location opens with a fade from black as Mira, Nell, Iris and Pip burst through the south doorway at a run, closely staggered, then continue into their investigations. Running uses twice the room walking speed and quicker step animation. The default six-tile fade and one-tile spacing bring everyone inside in about two seconds. Their landing poses keep the actual walking direction rather than forcing a south-facing turn before the scene. Residents such as Oren stay inside. Dialogue and reactions begin only after everyone has arrived. Revisiting a room repeats the arrival while keeping already-seen discussions complete; loading a saved room resumes it. The following party scene is skippable. The companions spread out and react at overlapping times as they approach distinct real props. Nell leads with patient expertise and a little impatience; fearless Iris investigates with a held magnifying glass and dry academic remarks. Excitable, easily frightened Pip runs toward a discovery, startles into a harmless stumble and fall, then gets back up. The tumble uses dedicated forward-stumble, face-down and push-up sprites for all four directions, anchored at his heels and blended without spinning the standing sprite. It is a visual running gag; his dialogue stays with the room and his worries rather than narrating the fall. Recognized unchanged stock gag lines in older saved projects update to the revised speech while custom text, authored timing and other edits remain intact. Mira is observant, plucky, brash and whimsical, with the biggest personality and a frequent interest in food. Their room-specific conversation connects what each person finds. Pixel question marks, lightbulbs and other reactions appear overhead. All 23 locations have authored party performances. Character-specific text bloops follow the revealed words, and Pip’s stumble and landing make small wooden knocks. The Sound switch controls these effects and rhythm playback together; pausing, seeking, skipping or closing a scene never causes a catch-up burst. Searching during a scene is visual only: it never collects notes, opens a door, reveals a hidden cache or records an inspection. Each spoken line waits for the player while the party continues its physical performance. Dialogue uses a prominent blue JRPG frame, large white block pixel lettering and a bold speaker name. Its letter-by-letter reveal pauses at punctuation, giving each line a speaking rhythm; long lines scroll inside a fixed three-line window. Click the dialogue box, use Next, or press E/Enter/Space: the first input reveals the remainder of the current line, and the next advances to the following line. Dialogue never disappears on a timer. Advancing text leaves walking and physical gestures continuous; after the last acknowledgement, any remaining walk or tumble finishes before exploration resumes. Skip/Escape returns to exploration. The camera reserves space above the dialogue box so it does not cover the cast. The 1×/2× control changes arrival and story playback speed without changing authored tile timing. Skipping during arrival puts everyone safely inside; pausing, leaving the tab, or closing a preview freezes or clears the fade with the walk. Settings can replay the room’s entrance. Completed or skipped scenes are remembered for the current run; Start over resets that memory. Final cast positions retain matching talk targets, hotspots and foot collision, with reachable alternative approaches to props. Saved run metadata keeps these poses separate from note transactions. Reduced motion keeps essential walking and stepping continuous, disables decorative bounce/pop animations, and uses a fixed wide view. Hiding or leaving the window pauses playback. Speaker handoffs ease the camera between characters. Sprite and emote movement uses compositor transforms; stationary controls, captions and room scale are not rewritten every frame. Layout measurements are cached until dialogue or viewport size changes.

The **Cutscene** editor gives each character four separate rows: **Walk**, **Text**, **Emote** and **Action**. Each item has its own draggable start and trim handle; moving or resizing a spoken line or reaction leaves walking unchanged. The tracks share a playhead and Play/Pause/Stop, so a character can walk, speak and react at the same time. Director previews play dialogue automatically at its authored times; normal play and Apply & playtest wait for dialogue input. Drag, trim, keyboard nudges and scrubbing snap to whole walking tiles: `one tile = room grid size / room walking speed` seconds (about 0.123 seconds at the default 32px/260px/s). A movement clip cannot be shortened below the time needed to reach its destination. Walk items can use a normal walk or a run at twice the room speed; both keep their cardinal, collision-aware paths. Click a character in the frozen Cutscene map to select their clip and show a colored arrow route. Drag its handles to reshape waypoints or move the endpoint; drag a route segment to insert a point, use arrows to nudge it one ground tile, or Delete an intermediate point. Routes remain collision-aware, and each drag is one Undo operation. Mira’s stationary endpoint can be dragged into a first walk. Clicking a moving character pauses the preview for editing. Select a Walk item to edit its character, route and action, a Text item to edit its speaker and words, an Emote item to edit its character and reaction, or an Action item to choose a magnifying glass, trip, fallen pose or recovery. Each item has its own start and duration. Add Walk, Text, Emote or Action creates just that item; Duplicate and Delete affect only the selection. Empty scripts leave the doorway arrival available without a discussion. Different rows can overlap freely; clips on the same character’s same row are safely scheduled in order. Existing full and four-room authoring projects gain only missing defaults, preserving their edits. The collapsed **Room entry** section controls whether the doorway arrival runs, its Walk/Run pace, fade length, party spacing and doorway X. Run is the default; explicit saved fade and spacing settings keep their authored values. **Preview room entry** plays the fade and arrival before the scene; regular Play and scrubbing go directly to the authored tracks. Arrival has its own clock and leaves all Walk, Text, Emote and Action start times unchanged. Saved/exported project JSON includes every separate item and the entry settings. Older combined clips are split automatically while preserving their compiled timing and authored content. Selecting Text, Emote or Action hides route handles; clicking a character on the map selects their Walk item.

Preview and scrubbing animate the frozen editing view without changing ordinary progress, inventory, pickups, discovered routes or the saved run. Leaving the timeline, changing rooms, closing the editor or stopping a preview restores the authoring view. `room-entry.mjs` supplies collision-safe doorway arrivals and their deterministic fade clock; `cutscene-content.mjs` supplies the discussions, `cutscene.mjs` compiles collision-safe paths and tile timing, and `cutscene-view.mjs` stages sprites, reactions and readable dialogue. `cutscene-editor.mjs` supplies the timeline, `cutscene-paths.mjs` supplies the route handles, and `jrpg-dialogue.mjs` supplies the static glyph rows and typewriter clipping. Press Start 2P and VT323 are bundled from Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License; see `assets/fonts/PressStart2P-OFL.txt` and `assets/fonts/VT323-OFL.txt`. No cinematic code grants game resources.

Press **F2**, or choose **Settings → Developer mode**, to freeze the actual room and open its inspector. Click a character to edit their opening dialogue and player replies; add, remove or rename choices and write their responses. Click a door to build its ordered answer from note tiles, or a chest to change its supplies with note counters. Room thumbnail cards switch the frozen editing view without paying a door or changing the ordinary journey. Drag a companion to move their sprite, click region, approach point and foot collision together. The canary in the depot’s glass roost now rests on its brass perch.

The inspector also edits lore, gifts, route connections, warnings, outcomes, room art, spawn points, walking speed, player/cast artwork, note labels/values/rests/subdivisions and playback tempo. Advanced JSON exposes the complete authoring project, including added rooms and custom endings. The nine supported engraved note IDs remain the notation catalog. Inputs are validated data and text, never evaluated code. Invalid imports leave the current draft intact. Undo/Redo and Restore shipped project operate on the draft. On phones, Expand editor gives text editing more space; Show map returns to the frozen scene.

**Save project** keeps the authoring draft on this device. **Export project** downloads `coda-quiet-bell-project.json`; **Import project** restores it, so edits can be shared for repository implementation. **Apply & playtest** saves the draft and starts a fresh isolated run with the edited rules. Test controls jump to any of the 23 playable locations, set exact note inventory and reset pickups. Closing the inspector resumes that test; **End playtest** restores the published run. Saving another draft does not replace the applied test until Apply is pressed. The editor never publishes changes to other players by itself. Older projects using the stock companion portraits automatically receive directional walking. Custom companion art can define `cast.<character>.walk` with a sprite image, cell dimensions, four directions and measured pose anchors; `walk: false` selects standing artwork.

Developer data uses `codaQuietBellDeveloperProjectV1` (saved draft), `codaQuietBellDeveloperAppliedProjectV1` (applied definition), `codaQuietBellDeveloperRunV1`, `codaQuietBellDeveloperJourneyV1`, and `codaQuietBellDeveloperActiveV1`. Ordinary progress, journal and Notes wallet are kept separate. `developer-project.mjs` supplies the portable schema, validation and edited journey catalog; `developer-console.mjs`/`.css` supply the scene inspector. The engine accepts explicit custom definitions and opt-in developer transactions; ordinary save parsing rejects those transactions. Edited locks use their actual total duration rather than a fixed four beats, and audio follows the edited note durations, rests, subdivisions and tempo.

Edit `game.html` and runtime source files, rather than generated `index.html`, `release.json` or `releases/`. Run the release builder after source changes and commit its output. It keeps earlier releases for cached sessions; the release test detects a forgotten rebuild. Recognized older four-room developer projects are upgraded to the full campaign while preserving existing edits. Custom unrelated definitions retain their own rules.
