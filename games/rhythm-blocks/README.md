# The Quiet Bell

The Quiet Bell is the dungeon-puzzle evolution of Rhythm Blocks. Its four playable rooms replace the falling interface at `games/rhythm-blocks/`; the URL, arcade game ID `rhythm-blocks`, and host key `rhythmBlocks` stay stable. Choose **Games → The Quiet Bell** in the arcade. It ships as static files and uses no runtime service or package installation.

The game fills the browser viewport without document scrolling. A wider camera keeps the full room height visible and centers the room when it fits. On narrow screens it follows Mira sideways across the room. Only the location, small square Inventory/Settings icons, and contextual interaction controls remain during exploration. Conversations, the bag, door puzzles, settings, and the arrival preview appear in temporary panels.

The rooms and characters use the selected Pocket Pixel style: compact proportions, large readable pixel clusters, warm dark outlines, simple faces, and a small palette. Mira keeps her blue bow, blue coat, and backpack; Nell her gray bun, glasses, and burgundy outfit; Iris her flowered garden hat and green overalls; Pip his teal cap, gold goggles, and satchel. The depot keeps its orthographic layout, parallel edges, three doors, post counter, music decorations, and delivery chest. The garden, workshop and canal rooms use distinct jade, copper-teal and indigo palettes while keeping that scale and projection. Their artwork and the transparent twelve-pose walking sheet are stored as lossless WebP assets. All rooms, furniture, Mira and the shared companion cast use orthographic 2/3 projection: tops and south faces are visible, parallel edges stay parallel, and floor spacing does not shrink into the distance. Smaller furnishings and 160-pixel character cells leave roughly 22–31% more reachable floor than the prior release. Measured boot anchors, actor depth and furniture silhouettes keep every sprite grounded.

The setting is the Lumen Reach: floating islands connected by a worn star-post network, living conduits, light canals, and inherited crystal machinery. The depot occupies a former sky-survey hall. Its warm returned mail, clockwork roost, sleeping dispatch screen, route orrery, and star-post receiver can be approached and inspected with E/Space or a click/tap. Each opens a short observation and keeps that discovered text in the journal across runs. These inspections leave note inventory, door payments, and the discovered map unchanged. Nell, Iris, and Pip accompany Mira in every playable room as separate, talkable NPCs. Each reacts to the current room and offers its route clue; Oren remains a local resident in the canal storehouse. The three companions share notes only at their authored gift stops: Nell in the depot, Iris in the garden, and Pip in the workshop. Conversation alone grants nothing. The later settling/departure routes and companion finales remain planned.

The room-by-room world guide in `design/music-puzzler/worldbuilding.json` and `worldbuilding.md` gives all fourteen authored locations a landmark, daily use, trace of the past, unanswered question, and inspection props. Four rooms are playable and ten remain planned. Iris’s porch has a mended second cup and a patient root graft; Pip’s workshop has a practice wing, fresh seed and an unmatched feather; Oren’s storehouse has drying postage seals, an inherited navigator and a mooring bell waiting for a reply. Nine new observations join the depot’s five without supplying notes or revealing untravelled chart connections.

The same game address checks for the latest published release whenever it opens or reloads. Each release keeps its HTML, scripts, styles, and artwork together under a content-versioned directory, so a cached arcade page or old version parameter cannot mix old and new game files. The address and origin stay unchanged, preserving local notes, discoveries, settings, and the arcade return protocol. The game never reloads itself during a puzzle and never clears browser storage. GitHub Pages publishing and CDN propagation can still take a few minutes; no manual cache clearing is needed after the updated launcher has loaded once.

Mira starts with an empty satchel in the Parcel Depot. Walk with arrows/WASD, click or tap the floor, or use the on-screen arrows on a touch device. Selecting a person, chest, or door makes Mira walk over and interact. E or Space interacts with the closest object in reach. Nell’s parcel and a door’s lock require Mira to be nearby. Collision uses her feet and prevents walking through walls, the counter, residents, and the chest.

Nell the postkeeper offers eight note types through conversation; the delivery chest contains the missing half note. Both can be collected once per run, in either order. Iris the gardener and Pip the courier have their own stories and clues about the room’s three physical doors. Approaching a resident opens a conversation; approaching a door opens its rhythm editor. Taking the parcel closes the conversation and returns to exploration. I or the Notes control opens a read-only bag; the actual editor is available at a door.

Choose a door and arrange its exact ordered four-beat key. The garden requires a half rest followed by a half note. The workshop requires a half note, paired eighths, then four sixteenths. The canal requires a half note, a quarter, then paired eighths. Hear a lock or a resident’s example without spending notes. Another four-beat rhythm does not open the selected lock and does not spend anything.

Opening a depot door consumes only its key and enters its actual porch room; unspent notes travel onward. Walking through a door gives no automatic notes. Iris and Pip each offer an explicit once-only four-beat gift; Oren points to a once-only stamped delivery chest. Each supply exactly matches its room’s forward lock. Every porch also has a visibly warned dead-end lock. The three forward gates currently stop at continuation previews for the next rooms; the overgrown arch, silent aviary and empty ferry are real dead-end outcomes with repair guidance. The balanced dungeon plan is in `design/music-puzzler/dungeon.json`; its verifier checks every reachable inventory and pickup order so all three regular and two secret endings use every note. Four explicit dead ends may retain notes. The later rooms, fourth dead end and hidden doors remain planned in that checked graph.

Undo returns a staged note. At a continuation preview or dead end, Revisit refunds that last lock while leaving that room’s collected supply claimed. Returning south from a porch rewinds its entry: the depot payment is refunded and the porch gift or chest is removed, so supplies cannot be carried back and farmed across branches. The depot’s parcel and chest remain claimed. Start over begins a new run with an empty satchel and resets run sources; permanent discoveries remain informational.

The pause screen draws a journey chart from rooms actually reached and connections actually travelled, accumulated across runs. It shows no unknown branches, placeholders, locked names, or completion totals. Reading a resident’s route clue adds it to the journal without revealing a place or connection on the chart. Successful locks record only their travelled connection and destination. Forward thresholds are continuation previews, while only observed dead ends enter the outcomes journal. Revisit, Start over, and reload preserve discoveries separately from the current run’s notes. Existing opening door discoveries migrate into the expanded chart.

The ultimate achievement, **The Complete Score**, requires every authored location, travelled connection, and outcome across runs, including secret endings and dead ends. The achievement stays disabled while the dungeon is unfinished; finding every shipped room and boundary cannot complete it. The deeper replay routes and companion finales remain planned in `design/music-puzzler/replay-and-party.md`.

Quarter notes, paired eighths, four sixteenths, and quarter rests each fill one beat. Half notes and half rests fill two, dotted halves fill three, and single eighths and paired sixteenths each fill half a beat. Rests sound as silence. There is no falling timer, combat, score, or penalty for listening and experimenting.

Click or tap people, doors, and the chest to approach them. While a lock is open, keys 1–9 choose note types in inventory order and Backspace undoes. Native Tab, Enter, and Space work with controls. Escape closes a panel or pauses during exploration. The settings icon opens the same pause screen, with the journey chart, discovered journal, Sound, How to play, Start over, and Return to arcade. The arrival preview also offers Your journey. Every open panel pauses movement; leaving the window, hiding the tab, and exiting clear held input. The arrival preview pauses movement until the answer is revisited. Sound starts only after an explicit action and reuses the existing sound preference. The return link preserves the arcade’s same-origin close protocol.

The device-only key `codaQuietBellPageOneV1` stores a version-3 action log of claimed sources and paid doors, plus the draft, conversation and room position. Valid version-2 opening runs migrate: a paid opening becomes an actual porch arrival without an arrival gift. A saved position is accepted only on the current room’s walkable floor; malformed or blocked positions use its spawn. Position is saved when movement stops or an interaction happens, not every animation frame. Reload reconstructs resources from actions rather than trusting saved balances. Invalid histories cannot duplicate a gift or forge a paid door. The earlier version-1 opening save starts a fresh run under these rules. Falling-game scores, shared profiles, arcade progress, and Notes balances remain untouched; cloud sync and currency rewards are not implemented.

`codaQuietBellJourneyV1` stores only observed IDs for places, connections, clues, and outcomes. `journey.mjs` filters them against a trusted catalog, supplies immutable discovered-only snapshots, and checks the completion predicate. Runtime uses `CHAPTER_JOURNEY_MANIFEST`: ten possible observed destinations, nine travelled keys, twenty-seven clue records and three real dead-end outcomes. These are authoring counts, never undiscovered UI totals. The legacy `OPENING_JOURNEY_MANIFEST` remains available for compatibility. `journey-view.mjs` renders only observed snapshots. Unknown future IDs and forged achievement records are ignored; labels and clue text come from the catalog.

`room.mjs` supplies data-driven room bounds, obstacles, targets, swept-circle movement, and pathfinding. `room-view.mjs` renders Mira, accepts keyboard/touch/floor navigation, frames the room with a wider camera, and reports nearby interactions without touching inventory. It schedules frames only while movement needs them. The transparent twelve-pose sheet is bundled as `assets/mira-walk.webp`; `mira.mjs` holds measured foot anchors. `cast.mjs` selects Nell, Iris, Pip and Oren from `assets/party-cast.webp`, and `party-content.mjs` merges room-specific companion conversations with local residents. NPCs use their own boot anchors and foot-depth ordering, while their interaction and collision data remain in `scenes.mjs`. Foreground copies of the room art preserve depth when Mira walks behind residents or the chest.

`dungeon-game.mjs` replays the chapter’s typed inventory and door transactions; `chapter.mjs` catalogs its four rooms, five sources, nine locks and six boundaries. `chapter-content.mjs` supplies room dialogue, observations and boundary copy; `scenes.mjs` supplies room geometry, interaction targets and foreground masks. `story.mjs`, `opening.mjs` and `lore.mjs` preserve the earlier opening behavior and text for migration and regression checks. `engine.mjs` retains the earlier falling-game engine and shared note catalog. `notation.mjs` supplies musical engraving; see its license/source files. `audio.mjs` synthesizes the true note subdivisions locally. Room artwork is bundled in `assets/parcel-depot.webp`, `garden-porch.webp`, `workshop-porch.webp` and `canal-porch.webp`.

Press **F2**, or choose **Settings → Developer mode**, to freeze the actual room and open its inspector. Click a character to edit their opening dialogue and player replies; add, remove or rename choices and write their responses. Click a door to build its ordered answer from note tiles, or a chest to change its supplies with note counters. Room thumbnail cards switch the frozen editing view without paying a door or changing the ordinary journey. Drag a companion to move their sprite, click region, approach point and foot collision together. The canary in the depot’s glass roost now rests on its brass perch.

The inspector also edits lore, gifts, route connections, warnings, outcomes, room art, spawn points, walking speed, player/cast artwork, note labels/values/rests/subdivisions and playback tempo. Advanced JSON exposes the complete authoring project, including added rooms and custom endings. The nine supported engraved note IDs remain the notation catalog. Inputs are validated data and text, never evaluated code. Invalid imports leave the current draft intact. Undo/Redo and Restore shipped project operate on the draft. On phones, Expand editor gives text editing more space; Show map returns to the frozen scene.

**Save project** keeps the authoring draft on this device. **Export project** downloads `coda-quiet-bell-project.json`; **Import project** restores it, so edits can be shared for repository implementation. **Apply & playtest** saves the draft and starts a fresh isolated run with the edited rules. Test controls jump to any playable room, set exact note inventory and reset pickups. Closing the inspector resumes that test; **End playtest** restores the published run. Saving another draft does not replace the applied test until Apply is pressed. The editor never publishes changes to other players by itself.

Developer data uses `codaQuietBellDeveloperProjectV1` (saved draft), `codaQuietBellDeveloperAppliedProjectV1` (applied definition), `codaQuietBellDeveloperRunV1`, `codaQuietBellDeveloperJourneyV1`, and `codaQuietBellDeveloperActiveV1`. Ordinary progress, journal and Notes wallet are kept separate. `developer-project.mjs` supplies the portable schema, validation and edited journey catalog; `developer-console.mjs`/`.css` supply the scene inspector. The engine accepts explicit custom definitions and opt-in developer transactions; ordinary save parsing rejects those transactions. Edited locks use their actual total duration rather than a fixed four beats, and audio follows the edited note durations, rests, subdivisions and tempo.

Edit `game.html` and the runtime source files, rather than generated `index.html`, `release.json`, or the contents of `releases/`. Before testing or publishing any runtime change, run `node games/rhythm-blocks/build-release.mjs`. It creates an immutable release, updates the launcher and current release manifest, and keeps earlier releases available for already open or cached sessions. Commit the generated files with their source change. The release test checks that the published bundle matches the current source, so a forgotten rebuild fails the checks. Ordinary game changes no longer need manual cache-version edits in the arcade or module imports.
