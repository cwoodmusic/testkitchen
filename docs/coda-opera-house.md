# Sydney Opera House in Coda Town

The Opera House is a movable, south-facing **Urban kit** landmark. Search for **Sydney Opera House** in Town Builder, place it, and use **Walk test**. Approach the central glass entrance and press **E**. Adding the asset does not change terrain or place anything elsewhere on the saved map.

The exterior’s floor plate and the concert hall both measure **4096 × 2560 world units**. The tall sail roofs have their own transparent padding, collision outline and depth slices. The sandstone approach stays walkable; warm windows and uplights use the town’s existing night-light renderer.

The enterable hall has fixed timber walls, stepped side galleries and red acoustic panels. Hanging ceiling shells, suspended reflectors and overhead parallax have been removed. A smaller silver-pipe organ sits inside the recessed red stage bay. There are 196 usable ground-floor seats with central, side and cross aisles. The raised stage has a broad staircase. Chairs, architectural tiers, performers and instruments have physical collision.

Press **E** at a seat to sit and watch. The camera stays fixed on the stage while seated. Press **E**, use **WASD**, or choose **Stand up** to return to the aisle and the walking camera. The entrance, **Go outside**, and **Escape** return to the town.

## Performances and music

The same shuffled playlist runs inside and outside. Orchestra recordings bring 52 performers including a conductor to the stage; early New Orleans-style jazz brings an eight-piece band; piano recitals feature historical portrayals of Chopin, Liszt or Debussy. Native character sprites have instrument-specific hand, bow and conducting movements. Resting arms are suppressed under every playing pose, so hands remain on the keys, fingerboards, valves or beaters. Percussionists stand or sit behind their drums and gong. The conductor uses down/in/out/up for four beats, down/out/up for three and down/up for two; 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 group eighth notes in threes. Each recording carries its score meter and a representative conducting-pulse tempo. Gestures follow the recording clock, but do not transcribe every rubato or accelerando in the performance. Switching recordings updates the pattern behind the closed curtain without respawning the ensemble. Formations persist between recordings of the same type and do not respawn with the camera. Between acts, the curtains close before the performers change, then retract completely as the next recording starts. No curtain border remains across the organ during a performance.

Eight locally bundled recordings provide about 28 minutes of music. Each recording plays completely before the playlist advances. The shuffle plays every track before repeating and avoids consecutive repeats across playlist cycles. Town sound is 2.5% of the selected master volume with a gentle 1.5 kHz low-pass filter, other interiors 0.3% with a 600 Hz low-pass filter, and the concert hall 100% with full clarity. Entering or leaving changes the gain smoothly without restarting the recording.

The **Concert music** controls offer Play/Pause, Mute, volume and **Programme & credits**. Browser playback begins after a real user gesture. The credits identify the actual recording performers separately from the historical stage characters, and offer **Next performance**. Pausing or muting remains your choice through scene changes. Removing the venue stops the town music; background tabs suspend it without losing the current position.

Full recording sources, attribution and processing details are in [opera-music-sources.md](opera-music-sources.md). The classical recordings use public-domain dedications, CC BY 3.0 or CC BY-SA 4.0; the two historic jazz selections are identified as **US public domain**, with that geographic scope preserved in the in-game credits.

## Rendering and zoom

The timber floor uses softly varied boards and filtered, cached scenery levels at the actual canvas pixel scale. Thin grain and repeating seams no longer alias into dark stripes when the concert view shrinks. Performers and usable chairs keep their separate native sprite rendering.

Large scenery and night bloom reuse bounded raster caches. Zoom changes release previous backing images after the current draw finishes; terrain-mask revisions invalidate reduced scenery after edits. Native wave displacement is generated by one worker with one request in flight, with an exact synchronous fallback when workers are unavailable. Full-map views retain raised lakes in the terrain bitmap once wave movement is below a backing pixel; closer views animate visible lake patches and cull offscreen patches.

`python3 tests/town-opera-return-browser.py` repeats concert/watch/exit and wheel zoom-out/zoom-in on an isolated copy of the apartment/plaza map. It checks recovered walking performance, bounded overview drawing, browser errors and unchanged map data. In the managed Chromium run, returns recovered approximately 58–60 FPS. Full-map display can still be slower on software graphics; its animation callback dropped from about 175 ms to about 9 ms in the profiled case. These timings are measurements, not a device-independent frame-rate guarantee.

`python3 tests/town-water-worker-browser.py` compares threaded wave frames pixel for pixel against the synchronous renderer, including loop boundaries and worker reuse.

## Validation

The concert model tests check every seat’s collision and route from the entrance, stage access and the exact shared floor size. Performer tests cover the actual programme’s ensemble identities, instrumentation, poses, depth ordering and cached native sprites. Audio tests cover shuffle order, gesture handling, bounded failed-track recovery, preferences, lifecycle cleanup and source/time continuity across scene changes.

Fixed-view checks verify that mouse and touch drags leave the seated camera unchanged, including reduced motion. Late native-art loads refresh the single background cache.

Run `python3 tests/town-opera-browser.py` for real keyboard walking and seating, stage changes, decoded audio, volume transitions, credits, frame timing and screenshots in an isolated browser save. The test never writes the supplied map source.
